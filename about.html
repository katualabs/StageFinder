About StageFinder

Why this site exists, where the information comes from and how far you can rely on it.

Why this site exists

Arriving in Nairobi, the matatu system can feel like a puzzle. There is no single map, stages are known by nicknames rather than street names, and the right matatu might be one street away from the wrong one.

StageFinder Nairobi puts the basics in one place: which road your destination is on, which CBD stage to go to, what the route number looks like and roughly what you should pay. It is built for newcomers: students, new workers and visitors who want to move around with confidence.

How the data was collected

This first version uses placeholder information put together from general knowledge of the CBD. Every stage location, every route-to-stage link and every fare is marked as unverified in the page source until it has been checked in person.

Verification is planned in three steps:

  1. Visit every stage on a weekday morning, a weekday evening and a weekend, and note the exact street position and nearby landmarks.
  2. Ask conductors and sacco officials which route numbers board there and what they charge at peak and off-peak times.
  3. Ride the routes and record the fare actually paid, so each range on the routes page reflects real trips.

If you spot something wrong, please send feedback. Local knowledge is the best source there is.

How the site is built

StageFinder is a static website made with HTML and CSS only. It works without JavaScript, loads quickly on slow connections, can be read in bright sunlight on a small phone and respects your device’s reduced-motion setting.

Disclaimer: fares are estimates

Fares on this site are estimates, not official prices. Matatu fares are set by each sacco and change with the time of day, the weather, fuel prices and demand. Always confirm the fare with the conductor before you board. StageFinder Nairobi is not linked to any sacco, the NTSA or the county government.
