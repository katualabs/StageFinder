The basics

How matatus work

Matatus are privately owned minibuses and vans that carry most of Nairobi’s commuters. Each one belongs to a sacco, a transport co-operative, whose name is painted on the side.

  • Routes start from fixed stages in the CBD. Each stage serves one part of the city, so finding the right stage is half the journey.
  • The route number and final destination are shown on the windscreen or on a board on the side. Check both before you get in.
  • Most matatus leave the stage only when they are full, so the wait can be short at peak times and longer at quiet times.
  • The conductor (often called the makanga) calls out the destination, collects fares and tells the driver when to stop.

Money

Paying the conductor

  1. Ask the fare before you sit down. Say where you are going, for example “Githurai, ngapi?” (“Githurai, how much?”).
  2. Pay when the conductor asks. This is usually soon after the matatu leaves the stage.
  3. Use cash or M-Pesa. Many matatus show a till or paybill number inside. If you pay by M-Pesa, show the conductor the confirmation message.
  4. Carry small notes and coins. Conductors often do not have change for large notes, especially early in the morning.
  5. Wait for your change. If the conductor is busy, they will usually bring it back before your stop. Ask politely if they forget.

Timing

Peak hours

Fares go up when demand is high. Expect to pay the peak fare at these times on weekdays:

  1. 6:30 to 9:00 Peak Morning rush into town
  2. 9:00 to 16:30 Off-peak Quieter, cheaper and easier to get a seat
  3. 16:30 to 20:00 Peak Evening rush out of town, long queues at stages
  4. After 20:00 Off-peak Fewer matatus; some routes charge more late at night

Fares also rise when it rains, on Friday evenings and before public holidays. Weekends are usually charged at the off-peak fare.

Stay safe

Staying safe

  • Keep your phone and wallet out of sight, especially near the window and the door.
  • Hold your bag on your lap rather than on the floor or the seat beside you.
  • Wear the seatbelt where there is one.
  • If a matatu looks overloaded or the driver seems reckless, it is fine to wait for the next one.
  • Note the sacco name and number plate if you need to report a problem or recover a lost item.
  • After dark, use busy, well-lit stages and avoid waiting alone.

Fit in

Etiquette

  • Greet the conductor; a simple “Sasa” or “Habari” goes a long way.
  • Move in to the back seats so others can board quickly.
  • Tell the conductor your stop early, then say “Shukisha hapa” (“Drop me here”) or knock twice on the roof as you approach it.
  • Keep phone calls short and use headphones for music and videos.
  • Give up front seats to older passengers, pregnant women and people with disabilities.

Learn the lingo

Matatu words

Makanga
The conductor who collects fares and calls out the route.
Stage
A boarding point. In the CBD each stage serves a particular part of the city.
Sacco
The co-operative that owns and runs a group of matatus on a route.
Nganya
A matatu decorated with bold art, lights and loud music.
Shukisha
“Drop me off”. Use it to ask the conductor to stop.
Ngapi?
“How much?” The quickest way to ask the fare.

FAQ

Questions newcomers ask

How do I know which matatu to take?

Find your direction on the routes page, note the route number and stage, then go to that stage. Check the number and destination on the windscreen, and ask the conductor if you are unsure.

Can I pay with M-Pesa?

Many matatus accept M-Pesa through a till or paybill number shown inside the vehicle. Cash is still the safest option, so carry some small notes as a backup.

Why was I charged more than the fare on this site?

Fares change with demand. Rush hour, rain, holidays and late nights all push fares up. The fares on this site are estimates, so the conductor’s price on the day is the one that counts.

What if I miss my stop?

Tell the conductor straight away and get off at the next stop. Cross the road and take a matatu going back the other way; short hops are usually cheap.

Are matatus safe at night?

Many people use them at night without problems. Stick to busy stages, keep valuables hidden, sit away from the door and consider a ride-hailing car for very late journeys.

I left something in a matatu. What can I do?

Go back to the stage the matatu left from and ask the sacco officials there. It helps a lot if you noted the sacco name or the number plate.
